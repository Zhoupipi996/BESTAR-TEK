OEM vs ODM Android Tablet Manufacturing:
What's the Real Difference?

📅 March 2025⏱ 7 min read✍ BESTAR TEK Business Team

Ask ten people in the hardware sourcing industry to define "OEM" and "ODM" and you'll likely get ten different answers. The terms are used interchangeably in casual conversation but mean fundamentally different things when it comes to contracts, IP ownership, design control, and what you're actually paying for.

If you're a software company looking to put your platform onto custom Android hardware, understanding the OEM/ODM distinction will directly affect your budget, your timeline, your IP position, and your long-term product flexibility.

1. The Textbook Definitions

ModelFull NameWho Designs the ProductWho Owns the Design
OEMOriginal Equipment ManufacturerThe client (you) specify / designThe client (you)
ODMOriginal Design ManufacturerThe manufacturer designsThe manufacturer

In a pure OEM arrangement, you come to the manufacturer with your design, specifications, or engineering package — they manufacture it to your spec. You own the design. In a pure ODM arrangement, the manufacturer already has a product design; you select from their catalog, add your branding, and sell it as your own.

The reality in 2025: Most commercial Android tablet and digital signage projects sit somewhere in between — they start with a manufacturer's existing hardware platform (ODM base) and layer significant client-specific firmware, ID design, and configuration customization on top (OEM elements). BESTAR TEK calls this "custom ODM" or "hybrid OEM/ODM."

2. What You Actually Get With Each Model

Pure ODM — Off-the-Shelf with Your Logo

The manufacturer has a product already in production. You select it from their catalog, they add your logo to the chassis and packaging, potentially load your app, and ship it. This is the fastest and cheapest route but offers minimal differentiation:

  • ✓ Fastest time-to-market (weeks, not months)
  • ✓ Lowest NRE (Non-Recurring Engineering) cost — often zero
  • ✓ Proven hardware — all bugs already found
  • ✗ No hardware differentiation from competitors using same factory
  • ✗ Limited firmware customization depth
  • ✗ You don't own the hardware design or PCB
  • ✗ Manufacturer can stop producing the base product

Custom ODM — The Most Common Commercial Approach

You select an existing hardware platform as the base, then work with the manufacturer to customize: enclosure color, button layout, port configuration, screen specification, battery capacity, firmware (full AOSP), branding, and packaging. This is what BESTAR TEK specializes in:

  • ✓ Significant product differentiation without full hardware design cost
  • ✓ Custom firmware (AOSP) — your app, your UX, your identity
  • ✓ Faster than full OEM (no PCB design phase)
  • ✓ MOQ-friendly — from 500 units at BESTAR TEK
  • ✓ Your brand, your packaging, your product in the market
  • ✗ Base hardware platform belongs to manufacturer
  • ✗ Some hardware constraints from existing platform

Full OEM — Truly Custom Hardware

You provide (or commission) the full hardware design: PCB layout, mechanical enclosure ID files, component BOM, and certification package. The manufacturer builds to your spec. This is reserved for companies with significant engineering resources and volume:

  • ✓ Complete hardware IP ownership
  • ✓ No hardware constraints — fully to your specification
  • ✓ Unique product impossible to copy from catalog
  • ✗ Requires hardware engineering team or expensive R&D partner
  • ✗ NRE costs: $50,000–$500,000+ depending on complexity
  • ✗ 6–18 month development timeline before mass production
  • ✗ Minimum volumes typically 10,000+ units to amortize NRE

4. IP Ownership: The Critical Question

IP ownership is where OEM/ODM decisions have the most long-term impact. Key questions to resolve in your manufacturing agreement:

  • Firmware source code: Who owns the AOSP customization your manufacturer writes? At BESTAR TEK, clients receive full source code delivery for custom firmware work.
  • Industrial design: If the manufacturer designs a custom enclosure for you, who owns those ID files?
  • Certification ownership: CE/FCC certificates are issued to a legal entity — ensure they're issued to or assigned to your company, not the manufacturer.
  • Non-compete / exclusivity: Can the manufacturer sell the same base product to your direct competitors? Negotiate exclusivity on key customizations if volume justifies it.
