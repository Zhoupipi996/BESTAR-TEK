RK3566 vs RK3588: Which Rockchip Chipset
Is Right for Your OEM Android Project?

📅 June 2025⏱ 8 min read✍ BESTAR TEK Engineering Team

If you're sourcing a custom Android tablet or commercial display from China, you've almost certainly encountered two chipset names: Rockchip RK3566 and Rockchip RK3588. Both are popular in the OEM/ODM market — but they serve fundamentally different project profiles. Choosing the wrong one will either leave you paying for performance you don't need, or shipping a device that can't handle your software's demands.

This guide breaks down both chipsets from a commercial OEM perspective: architecture, real-world performance, Android version support, connectivity, and — critically — where each makes sense for your specific use case.

Bottom line up front: RK3566 is the right choice for 80% of commercial OEM projects — it's cost-efficient, well-supported, and more than powerful enough for most business applications. RK3588 is worth the premium only when you need genuine high-performance compute, 8K output, or advanced AI inference.

1. Architecture Overview

Both chips are manufactured by Rockchip (Fuzhou Rockchip Electronics Co., Ltd.) — a leading Chinese fabless semiconductor company with deep experience in the Android embedded market. Here's how they compare at the architecture level:

2. Real-World Performance for Commercial Applications

Benchmark numbers matter less than real-world performance under your software's load. Here's how both chips perform across common commercial OEM scenarios:

RK3566 — Strengths in Commercial Deployments

The RK3566 handles the vast majority of business Android applications without any perceptible lag. It excels at:

  • Meeting room booking displays — calendar sync, LED control, lightweight web apps
  • Digital signage playback — Full HD video loops, image carousels, dynamic content
  • Education tablet apps — reading apps, quiz platforms, video lessons at 1080p
  • Smart home control panels — IoT dashboards, device control, automation interfaces
  • POS and kiosk terminals — order entry, customer-facing menus, queue management
  • Healthcare data entry — patient forms, clinical record apps, moderate image viewing

With 4 GB RAM and 32 GB storage in a typical OEM configuration, the RK3566 runs Android 11/13 smoothly for all of the above applications.

RK3588 — When the Extra Power Pays Off

The RK3588 is genuinely in a different performance tier. Its Cortex-A76 cores are approximately 3× more powerful per-core than the Cortex-A55 in the RK3566. This matters for:

  • Multi-window multitasking — running multiple apps simultaneously with no frame drops
  • Real-time AI/ML inference — on-device face recognition, OCR, medical image analysis
  • 4K video recording and editing — media production tablets
  • Multi-display signage controllers — driving 2–4 screens simultaneously
  • High-end gaming or simulation — training simulation, interactive product demos
  • Large-format touch displays 21"–32" — where rendering a complex UI at high resolution demands more GPU

3. Android Version & Long-Term Software Support

For OEM projects, Android version support directly affects your product lifecycle. Here's what to expect:

  • RK3566: Officially supported on Android 11 and Android 13. A large ecosystem of BSP (Board Support Package) vendors, strong community, and proven production track record since 2021. Most OEM manufacturers including BESTAR TEK stock RK3566 in volume.
  • RK3588: Supports Android 12 and Android 13 with Rockchip's official SDK. As an 8nm chip launched in 2022, its BSP is mature but the ecosystem of ODM vendors supporting it is smaller than RK3566. Better suited for projects with longer planned product lifespans needing future Android 14 paths.
Important for OEM projects: If your software currently targets Android 11 or 12, either chip works. If you're building for Android 14 and beyond, discuss the upgrade roadmap with your manufacturer before locking in the chipset.

5. Cost Impact on Your OEM Project

In volume OEM production, chipset cost flows through to your unit price more significantly than most buyers expect. At 1,000 units:

  • An RK3566-based 10.1" commercial display might be priced at $55–85 per unit depending on configuration
  • The equivalent RK3588 version typically adds $18–28 per unit to the BOM
  • At 1,000 units, that's $18,000–$28,000 in additional investment — which requires a clear justification in your product's feature set or target price point
